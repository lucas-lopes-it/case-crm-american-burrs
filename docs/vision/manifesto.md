# Manifesto

> Dashboards demand attention. Briefings give it back.

## What I believe

Sales reps don't need more information. They need less to read before acting.

Correct data that nobody checks is not an adoption problem. It is a product that demands attention
the user doesn't have.

Herbert Simon described this in 1971: a wealth of information creates a poverty of attention, and a
system only relieves its users when it absorbs more information than it produces.

## AI is not the product

The AI is the one who drafts. The product is the decision about when it writes, about what, within
which limits and who reads it before it is sent.

That is why the product stays standing when the model fails: a template with the account data takes
over, and the failure is logged.

## The rule existed before the AI

The most important rule in the briefing wasn't born for the model. Reps didn't give discounts, so
the AI doesn't either.

Before asking what the AI can do, it is worth asking what the business has already decided nobody
does.

## Order before tools

First understand the buyer's cycle. Then validate the riskiest hypothesis on an existing platform.
Only then build.

The product went through three tools. The hypothesis was the same from start to finish.

## What I don't measure, I flag

A metric without instrumentation appears in the README as "to be instrumented", not as an outcome.
What was measured in production and what only exists in the evals stay visibly separate.

## Mistakes stay in the repository

This product was rebuilt with a coding agent. Every deviation from the approved plan is in
[AGENT_LOG.md](../AGENT_LOG.md), with the rule that applied from then on. Supervising the agent is
part of the job, and the log is the proof that it was done.
