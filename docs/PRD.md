# PRD: Next best action for the academic sales rep

> PRD rebuilt for the portfolio from the product that ran in production at American Burrs. The
> outcome numbers are in the [README](../README.md#outcome); the decisions, with alternatives and
> accepted cost, in [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md).

## Context

American Burrs sells dental burs to dental schools. The decision maker is the professor, who builds
the class kit every academic term. The ERP record can be under the school's name or the
professor's.

The ERP already classified each account by color, based on days since the last purchase. Nobody
checked it. Reps didn't like filling in the CRM, and the company paid for two commercial tools (CRM
and sales force automation) to store data that arrived incomplete.

## Problem

The rep carries alone the decision of who to talk to and what to say. The information for that
exists, but it sits in a place they don't open, and turning it into action costs attention they
don't have. The result is lost repurchases that nobody notices in time.

## Users

| User | Job | Pain |
|---|---|---|
| Sales rep | Keep the portfolio buying every term | Doesn't know who is cooling down and has no time to prepare the outreach |
| Professor (customer) | Build the class kit on time | Being contacted late, after already closing with another supplier |
| Sales leadership | Track the portfolio and the cost of the operation | Depends on manual reports and pays for tools the team doesn't use well |

## Goals

1. The rep acts on the right day of the professor's cycle, without opening a dashboard.
2. End manual data entry in the CRM and in kits.
3. Reduce commercial tool costs.

## Out of scope

- Automatic message sending. Every text goes through the rep.
- Discounts, promotions or commercial terms generated by the AI.
- Predictive churn score. The days-since-last-purchase rule was already understood and accepted by
  sales.
- Screens inside the ERP.

## Riskiest hypothesis

The rep will act if the account arrives already read, with the next action and the message ready,
in the place where they already work. If this hypothesis fails, nothing else matters. That is why it
was tested first, before building any application of our own.

## Requirements

### Traffic light and trigger
- R1. Compute the traffic light per account every day from sales history, with the ERP bands:
  green (1 to 30 days), yellow (31 to 60), red (61 to 90), black (91 to 120), purple (no purchase or
  more than 120).
- R2. Show the traffic light inside the CRM, on the account, without the rep opening the ERP.
- R3. Generate the briefing when the account turns red (anticipate the next kit) or black (win back
  whoever skipped a cycle).

### Briefing
- R4. Deliver four parts: account situation, next action, WhatsApp message and email.
- R5. Cite only numbers that exist in the account history, including days without purchase and the
  last order.
- R6. Offer no discount, promotion, gift or special terms.
- R7. WhatsApp up to 50 words with a single question at the end; email up to 90 words.
- R8. Start as a draft. The rep reviews and sends in one click, via WhatsApp or email, and the send
  is logged as an activity in the CRM.
- R9. If the AI fails, deliver a template briefing with the account data, and log the failure
  (reconstruction requirement).

### Kit
- R10. Build the kit per class with price and stock read from the ERP.
- R11. Validate before registration: minimum of 8 units per student, at least one diamond bur and
  one carbide bur, stock for the whole class and cost per student within the ceiling.
- R12. Track the opportunity in a 5-stage pipeline: contact or visit, kit building, kit validation,
  in registration, kit registered.

## Success metrics

| Metric | Type |
|---|---|
| Red accounts that repurchase in the following term | North star |
| Time between an account turning red and first contact | Input |
| Briefings sent without edits | Quality |
| Briefings that fell back to the template | Health |
| Briefings with invented numbers or discounts (evals) | Guardrail |
| Commercial tool costs | Business |

## Phases

| Phase | What shipped | What it validated |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Lean Inception across four business areas | The professor as decision maker and the term-based cycle |
| MVP | Traffic light and briefing on top of a market CRM, with integrations built from scratch | Whether reps act on a ready-made briefing |
| Own product | CRM, pipeline and kit builder in our own low-code application | That the CRM license could be dropped and the sales force tool replaced |
| Public reconstruction | Rules in code, tests and AI evals, with fictional data | That the product rules are testable and versioned |

## Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| The AI invents a number or promises a discount | Rule in the prompt, runtime validation and an eval rubric |
| A wrong message reaches a professor | Mandatory human review |
| A school-level account hides a professor who skipped the cycle | Recorded as the cost of the decision; next step is treating the professor as the decision-making contact |
| WhatsApp to an inactive customer outside the API's 24-hour window | In the reconstruction, an approved template for the first message |
| The AI becomes unavailable | In the reconstruction, capped retries and a template with the account data |
